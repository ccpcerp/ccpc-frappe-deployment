## 0. Confirmed operating practice and lean identity control — 2026-10-14

**Further owner clarification:** Approximately five Stockroom staff each have their **own** ERPNext account. **Stockroom staff do not create Withdrawal Slips on behalf of other departments.** Most ordinary CCPC employees have no ERPNext account; their **supervisor creates/prepares a Slip on their behalf**, entering the actual employee's name in the existing free-text `requestor` field. Stockroom staff who need supplies for themselves must prepare their **own** Slip under their individual account. They may still self-approve under the existing Requestor-owned Draft→Approved transition. A **different named and authenticated Stockroom account** must review/release and post the Material Issue; the Stockroom manager is not a routine approver.

**Lean implementation design selected for future review (NOT IMPLEMENTED):** Compare the authenticated issuer `frappe.session.user` against both the saved `Withdrawal Slip.owner` (document creator) and immutable `approving_supervisor` (misnamed field actually labeled **Prepared By**). **Reject** where issuer matches either. Both values are account-linked identities available in the current schema; a new `requestor_user` Link field is **not presently necessary** for the confirmed process, so avoid avoidable schema/migration work. Lock/select `owner` and `approving_supervisor` with the other Withdrawal Slip fields and check for inconsistencies or missing creator identity (fail closed and investigate, never silently permit). Scope to slips linked to Material Issues, not all unrelated Stock Entries. The legacy draft endpoint must also reject a same-person issue to avoid creating an actionable draft; Stock Entry `before_submit` must recheck the same condition for alternate submission routes. No default Administrator/System Manager bypass without separate explicit emergency policy review.

**Assurance boundary:** This is a **different preparer-versus-issuer** control, which is adequate for the described workflow because Stockroom staff create their own requests and supervisors prepare slips for employees without accounts. It does **not** authenticate the free-text `requestor` name or actual receiver; where supervisors prepare on behalf, preserve both identities and do not mislabel the supervisor as the consuming employee. Staff with Stockroom accounts must not evade the rule by asking others to originate slips for their personal use. The owner rule is a procedural control plus server guard, not complete protection against deliberate false requester names, privileged reassignment of `owner`/Prepared By, account sharing, or bypass through unrestricted general-purpose Stock Entries. Those risks require audits, restrictions and documented future policy if warranted.

## 1. Business operation — agreed examples

| Scenario | Request/preparation | Approval per existing workflow | Stockroom issue and recording | Allowed under Option A? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Employee without an ERPNext account asks for office supplies | Their supervisor prepares the slip, recording the employee's name as Requestor | Supervisor uses existing Requestor transition | A separate authorized Stockroom employee posts | Yes; normal issue |
| Stockroom employee Juan needs two ballpens | Juan creates own slip | Existing Requestor transition; no routine manager | Another Stockroom employee, Pedro, checks, releases and posts | Yes |
| Stockroom employee Juan needs two ballpens | Juan creates own slip | Existing Requestor transition | Juan attempts to issue and post that slip | **No**; reject in server-side validation |
| Juan requests for himself, Pedro begins review, Maria issues | Juan, on his own ERPNext account | Existing workflow | Maria releases/posts | Yes, provided issuer really is different from Juan |
| Exception: only one Stockroom employee available | That employee may prepare slip | Existing workflow | **Leave unissued** until a different authorized employee can verify/post; a documented exception process requires a separate future policy | No routine bypass |

The peer issuer must act under **their own ERPNext user account**, inspect the request and quantity, record receipt accurately, and submit the linked Material Issue. Shared logins or pretending to be another user defeat the audit control. This policy does not prevent a Stockroom employee from being a requester.

**Important distinction:** Existing `Approve & Send to Stockroom` is a **Requestor-owned self-approval transition**, not an independent supervisor authorization. Option A deliberately leaves this routine transition in place and adds a different-person physical issue/posting control. Whether to rename the transition to avoid misleading 'approval' language is a separately reviewed usability choice.

## 3. Proposed enforceable server-side design — NOT IMPLEMENTED

**Design target requiring separate implementation authorization later:** A user submitting a linked Material Issue must **not be the authenticated creator/preparer** of that Withdrawal Slip. This directly fits CCPC's confirmed practice: Stockroom employees create their own slips and use personal ERPNext accounts; supervisors prepare slips for employees who lack accounts. A peer issuer acts under a different account. The editable `requestor` and `received_by` text fields continue to identify the human requester/receiver operationally but are not authentication evidence.

1. **Reuse existing authenticated originator**: On document creation, Frappe's `owner` and the existing read-only User Link `approving_supervisor` (**Prepared By**) already identify the authenticated preparer. A Stockroom employee must prepare their own Slip under their own account; an ordinary employee without an ERPNext login asks a supervisor to prepare theirs. No new Link User field or schema change is needed merely to distinguish the preparer from issuer under this confirmed practice. Review whether the user can tamper with `owner`, and ensure `approving_supervisor` remains server-stamped and immutable.
2. **Enforce different accounts for one Slip**: Use a shared server-side assertion rejecting `frappe.session.user` when it matches saved `owner` **or** `approving_supervisor`; both must be retrieved authoritatively from the locked Slip and checked consistently. Treat missing/inconsistent source identity as a security anomaly to resolve before posting. For supervisor-prepared slips, that supervisor is **not** assumed to be the consuming employee; text `requestor` remains a display/business field only. Do **not** compare arbitrary displayed names or allow editing `owner`, Prepared By or Received By to bypass the check.
3. **Single locked source of truth**: Expand the current parent `FOR UPDATE` read to include `owner` and `approving_supervisor` alongside the status and issuing fields, and compare with the freshly loaded Slip before allowing a posting (avoid stale REPEATABLE READ snapshots). If either originator ID is missing, stale or inexplicably inconsistent, **fail closed** and audit rather than posting. Keep the guard inside the normal request transaction without manual commit.
4. **All entry points**: Run a shared peer-issue assertion after the role + Slip write check and **before any privileged insert** in `issue_withdrawal_slip` and legacy `make_material_issue`; invoke it **again in the Stock Entry `before_submit` hook** for manually constructed/altered linked Stock Entries. `validate` may provide early feedback but cannot replace `before_submit` as a final backstop. For Administrator/System Manager bypass or recovery, require a separately reviewed audited emergency policy; do **not** silently exempt powerful roles.
5. **Audit trace**: Preserve Slip `owner` and `approving_supervisor` (creator/Prepared By), free-text business `requestor` (actual employee who may have no login), `received_by` (physical recipient), and authenticated `issued_by` from linked Stock Entry submission as **distinct facts**. Do not infer that typed employee names represent ERPNext users or independent approvers. Protect account-linked originator fields against unauthorized edits on the server, not only through UI `read_only` flags.
6. **Workflow UX**: Requestor may still use `Approve & Send to Stockroom` without manager; a colleague may use `Start Review`, adjust quantity/shortage notes and `Issue Materials`. Optionally hide Issue Materials for creator as convenience, but **server rejection is mandatory**. Determine whether the requester should be prohibited from `Start Review` as well; Option A explicitly requires a **different issuer**, and blocking self-review is optional defense-in-depth needing a separate review.

### Source-review improvement: require real Stockroom role, not inherited administrator access (2026-10-14)

The existing `_require_stockroom_user()` in `withdrawal_slip_stock.py` explicitly allows `Administrator` and `System Manager` even if not assigned the `Stockroom Personnel` role. The owner-approved Option A normally calls for **a different Stockroom employee**, not any differently named privileged user. A simple authenticated `issuer != owner` comparison does **not** enforce that role requirement. Therefore the future shared peer guard should also verify that the issuing account has the actual `Stockroom Personnel` role. This strict check must apply to linked stock issues through both scoped/legacy endpoints and the `before_submit` hook; an Administrator/System Manager account should not receive an automatic bypass from the peer guard merely because it passes legacy `_require_stockroom_user()`. Any recovery exception should be a separate, owner-approved, audited process rather than a hidden override.

A revised **offline-only review patch V2** adds that role check and 14 proposed mocked regression tests (including disallowing a System Manager without the Stockroom role); its content is only a sandbox artifact and is not a committed application change. The patch passed `git apply --check` and `git apply` on **synthetic preimage fragments**, Python AST parsing for the added guard/test module, and 11 isolated direct guard behavior checks. The full app/Frappe test suite and actual full-source checkout apply check are unverified. The developer must review whether system Administrators implicitly possess all roles in the target Frappe version before asserting this check alone is a complete privilege barrier; direct unlinked Stock Entry permissions remain separate.

### Offline patch v3 hardens the Administrator exception (2026-10-14)

Final local review of the previous v2 candidate observed that in some Frappe Administrator contexts, effective roles may be broader than ordinary explicit role assignments. Merely checking `frappe.get_roles(issuer)` for `Stockroom Personnel` could therefore permit Administrator to issue a Slip prepared by someone else. A **v3 offline-only candidate** explicitly disallows the built-in `Administrator` and `Guest` account, requires the `Stockroom Personnel` role on an authenticated non-Administrator issuer, and rejects same `owner`/Prepared By and missing/mismatched creator IDs. Real Stockroom personnel with other compatible roles may issue only if different from the creator; System Manager role alone does not authorize the peer check. This is a reviewed policy interpretation, not proof of effective Frappe permissions or a committed code fix.

## 4. Risk and negative-path review before any implementation

- **Supervisor on behalf of employee:** A supervisor's ERPNext account is the Slip preparer (`owner` and Prepared By); the human requesting supplies may have no login and is recorded as text `requestor`. Compare issuer with authenticated **preparer**, not with this unverified text. The rule is deliberately applicable to all Slip-bound issues, including those the supervisor prepares. Stockroom staff are explicitly **not** authorized by operating policy to have supervisors originate their own personal requests in order to evade peer issue.
- **Workflow bypass and direct submit:** Issue endpoints and Stock Entry `before_submit` must both reject same-person issuance; verify no alternate linked or unlinked Stock Entry bypass. **Unlinked general-purpose Stock Entries** are governed by standard ERPNext stock permissions; this policy cannot by itself prevent misuse of those rights, so avoid granting broad Stock User/Manager to routine Stockroom staff and audit who possesses it.
- **Identity tampering:** UI read-only state, custom requestor text and Received By text cannot authenticate identity; check `owner`, `approving_supervisor`, any new Link/User IDs, and change history server-side. A malicious direct API request must not substitute a colleague's identity.
- **Concurrency/accounting:** Keep existing row-level lock + submitted issue recheck, regular ERPNext stock/GL validations and single transaction; reject failed assertions before any `ignore_permissions`-assisted insertion, and use `before_submit` as backstop. Mock tests do not replace live MariaDB row lock and end-to-end rollback checks.
- **Legacy slips and migrations:** Under the confirmed process, no new schema field is necessary for the current preparer-versus-issuer rule. Legacy slips should already expose the standard `owner` and Prepared By user field, but their completeness and consistency must be validated before enabling enforcement. Fail closed for missing/inconsistent identity; do not try to backfill identity from free-text names or run migrations without explicit approval.
- **Audit of personal access:** Two Stockroom identities must be independently authenticated and must not share passwords. Test role auto-assignments and verify no unintended Stock Entry create/submit via combined roles. No new production access or role changes planned.

## 5. Future acceptance criteria (not tested yet)
